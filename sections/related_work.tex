\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learning-based spoofing detection}
\label{sec:rw_learning}

Signal-processing anti-spoofing tests operate on quantities the tracking loops
already expose. Automatic gain control and received-power monitoring flag the power
advantage that many spoofers introduce \citep{akos2012gnss}, and correlator-domain
tests together with checks on the consistency of Doppler and code measurements target
the distortion a counterfeit signal leaves on the correlation function
\citep{psiaki2016gnss}. Each test is tied to one physical signature and can be evaded
by an attacker who holds that signature within its nominal range, which motivated the
move to detectors that learn a joint decision across many observables.

Data-driven detectors now span both deep and classical model families. Among deep
models, \citet{borhanidarian2024deep} classify spoofing from cross-ambiguity-function
images with a parallelized deep network and report strong detection at moderate to high
signal-to-noise ratio, \citet{sung2022cnn} apply a one-dimensional convolutional network
to signal-strength features, \citet{romaniuc2025lstm} train a long
short-term memory model on receiver-independent exchange data and report an $F_1$ near
0.90, and \citet{yang2025deep} combine a bidirectional recurrent network, attention
and convolution for a small uncrewed platform and report a weighted $F_1$ of 0.97.
Among classical models, \citet{chen2022svm} use a support vector machine,
\citet{mahroof2024ml} use $k$-nearest neighbours on the TEXBAT ds3 and ds8 scenarios,
and \citet{jullian2022deep} compare a multilayer perceptron with a recurrent model.
Reported detection accuracy in this line of work is consistently high, which reflects
evaluation on a fixed set of attacks with the training and test data drawn from the
same conditions.

\subsection{Generalization and physics-informed detection}
\label{sec:rw_generalization}

A separate concern is whether a detector trained on one set of attacks still works on
attacks it has not seen. \citet{iqbal2024deep} motivate a one-class framework by the
observation that supervised detectors fail on attack vectors absent from the training
distribution, and \citet{chen2025cgan} address unknown scenarios by augmenting
engineered features with a conditional generative model before classification.
\citet{li2025realtime} target real-time time-spoofing detection through a
feature-processing pipeline. \citet{song2026generalized} report that standard learned
detectors lose accuracy on unseen spoofing scenarios, and that embedding
the code-carrier consistency of authentic signals as a training constraint keeps an
accuracy retention rate above 0.92 across ten held-out scenarios, establishing that a
physical invariant used as an inductive bias improves generalization across attack
conditions on clean signals. Robustness to an adversary that adapts the spoofing signal
against the detector is a distinct axis, and it is the axis this study evaluates.

\subsection{Adversarial robustness and gradient masking}
\label{sec:rw_adversarial}

Machine-learning classifiers can be driven to a wrong decision by a perturbation that
is small under the chosen norm \citep{szegedy2014intriguing}. Gradient methods
construct such perturbations efficiently, from the single-step sign method
\citep{goodfellow2015explaining} to iterative projected gradient descent
\citep{madry2018towards} and the optimization attack of
\citet{carlini2017evaluating}. Perturbations often transfer between models, so an
attacker without access to the target can craft examples on a surrogate
\citep{papernot2016transferability,liu2017delving}. When the target exposes no useful
gradient, decision-based attacks reach comparable strength using only the hard output
label \citep{brendel2018decision,chen2020hopskipjump}. This last point is central to a
fair comparison across model families. A non-differentiable model such as a tree
ensemble or a nearest-neighbour classifier resists gradient attacks by construction,
and reading that resistance as robustness is the error that
\citet{athalye2018obfuscated} name gradient masking. The recommended practice is to
attack every model with a method matched to its interface, including a gradient-free
attack, before any robustness claim \citep{carlini2019evaluating}.

Adversarial examples have been demonstrated against machine-learning systems that
consume physical measurements, including audio \citep{carlini2018audio}, automotive
sensor fusion \citep{cao2021invisible}, synthetic-aperture radar
\citep{lin2023sar} and power-system monitoring \citep{cheng2022adversarial}. In each
setting the attacker cannot write arbitrary values into the feature vector and must
instead induce them through a physical channel, so a credible evaluation constrains
the perturbation to what that channel can produce. The same requirement applies to
GNSS, where the spoofer controls the transmitted signal but not the receiver
observables directly.

\subsection{Adversarial evaluation in the GNSS setting}
\label{sec:rw_gap}

Adversarial robustness of GNSS spoofing detectors is less explored than detection
accuracy. \citet{an2025adversarial} demonstrate evasion of a support vector machine
operating on reported position, showing that a detector with high nominal accuracy can
be defeated by an adversary that shapes its input, and identify the robustness of other
detector architectures under black-box conditions as an open question.

The evaluation reported here addresses that question directly. It covers fourteen
detectors spanning both families, places them at a shared operating point, applies one
attack suite to all of them including a gradient-free decision-based attack that
measures vulnerability without relying on gradients, and projects every adversarial
example onto the band set by the coupling between reported signal quality and
correlator power, the realizability condition detailed in Section~\ref{sec:enforcer}.
